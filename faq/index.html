FAQ

Every walk-in tub and roll-in shower question, answered

What is the biggest mistake people make when buying a walk-in tub?

Choosing features first and fit second. Seat height, step-in height, door style and the size of the space decide whether you will use the tub every day. Get those right, then add comfort features you know you will use.

Are walk-in tubs only for older adults?

No. People recovering from hip or knee surgery, people with arthritis or balance problems, and families caring for a loved one all choose walk-in tubs. Some buyers are simply planning ahead so bathing stays safe as they age.

Will a walk-in tub fit where my old bathtub is?

Often, yes. Most walk-in tubs fit the same 60 by 30 to 32 inch space as a standard tub, and compact models around 48 to 53 inches long are available. We confirm the drain location and the condition of the space before anything is ordered.

How much open floor space do I need in front of a walk-in tub?

Enough for the door to open fully and for you to stand, turn and sit without bumping the toilet or vanity. Measure from the front of the tub to the nearest fixture or wall, and send us a photo. Door style choices can help in tight bathrooms.

What is a dual fast-drain system?

It is a walk-in tub drain setup with two drains, or a larger drain, so the tub empties much faster than a single standard drain. Because you stay seated while a walk-in tub drains, a faster drain means less time sitting wet as the water cools.

Is hydrotherapy worth paying extra for?

It is worth it if you will use it often. Many people find warm water and jets soothing for sore joints and muscles, but if your main goal is a safe way in and out, a simpler soaking tub may serve you just as well. Ask your doctor about heat, blood pressure and circulation first.

Will a walk-in tub raise my water bill?

It can, compared with quick showers. How much depends on how deep you fill it and how often you bathe. Choosing a tub sized for your body, not the biggest one available, keeps water use reasonable.

Can a walk-in tub help if my knees or hips are stiff?

It removes the high tub wall you would otherwise step over and lets you sit at chair height instead of lowering yourself to the floor of the tub. Grab bars placed for your reach make sitting and standing easier. A walk-in tub is a bathing fixture, not a medical device.

What does my own contractor need from you for a supply-only install?

Your contractor needs the product specs, rough-in measurements, the drain side and any electrical needs for jets or a heater. We send these before delivery so your installer can plan the plumbing and wiring. In Arizona that contractor should hold an ROC license.

What should I measure before I ask for a quote?

Measure the length and width of the current tub or shower, the width of every doorway on the path from the front door, and the distance from the tub to the toilet and vanity. Note which end the drain is on. A few phone photos help as much as the numbers.

Are there walk-in tubs for taller people with long legs?

Yes. Longer and deeper models give more legroom, and some have wider seats. Tell us your height and how you like to sit in a bath, and we will narrow the list to models that fit you and your space.

Can someone who uses a wheelchair transfer into a walk-in tub?

Some people can, using a seat at the right height, a wide door and well-placed grab bars. But every walk-in tub still has a threshold and needs a seated transfer. If transfers are hard or unsafe, a roll-in shower with a fold-down seat is usually the better choice.

How does a walk-in tub door keep water in?

When the door is latched, it presses firmly against a rubber gasket that seals the opening. Correct installation and a level tub matter as much as the seal itself. Ask how the door seal is covered under the warranty.

What should a walk-in tub warranty cover?

Look for clear coverage on the door seal, the tub shell and surface, and the mechanical parts such as jets, pumps and an inline heater. Ask how long each part is covered, who does the repair and whether the warranty transfers if you sell your home.

Can I use a VA grant and pay the rest myself?

Yes. A VA HISA grant pays up to its lifetime limit, and you can cover the balance yourself. A VA physician must prescribe the change first. We provide the itemized quote and product details the application asks for.

What matters most if I have weak hands or arthritis in my fingers?

Look for lever-style or large controls instead of small knobs, a door handle that is easy to grip and grab bars you can reach without twisting. Tell us about any hand weakness so we can rule out models with hard-to-use controls.

What is the quickest way to narrow down my choices?

Take the two-minute Fit Check, then call 1-888-779-2284. A short conversation about how you bathe and the size of your space usually narrows the choices to a few models. You can take as long as you like from there.

Does a walk-in tub need its own electrical circuit?

Tubs with jets, an inline heater or a pump usually need power, and many need a dedicated circuit with GFCI protection installed by a licensed electrician. A basic soaking tub without jets may need no power at all. Your quote will say what your model requires.

What happens if the power goes out while I am in the tub?

Ask how your model drains without power before you buy. A gravity drain still empties when the power is out, while a pump-assisted drain may not. Jets and heaters stop, but the door and the seat work the same.

Do walk-in tubs come in left-hand and right-hand versions?

Many models do. The drain and faucet end should match where the plumbing already is, which usually keeps installation simpler and the cost lower. Look at which end your current tub drains from and tell us when you call.

Is a roll-in shower easier for a caregiver who helps someone bathe?

Usually, yes. A caregiver can stand outside the spray, roll a shower chair straight in and use a handheld shower without lifting anyone over a wall or into a tub. A fold-down seat and well-placed grab bars help both people.

Do you remove my old tub as part of installation?

Yes. With full installation, our crew removes the old tub or shower, installs the new one, connects the plumbing and cleans up. Your written quote spells out what is included, so ask about disposal of the old fixture if you have questions.

Does my home bathroom have to meet ADA rules?

No. The Americans with Disabilities Act covers public places and businesses, not private homes. Its guidelines are still a useful reference for grab bar height and turning space, and we can follow them where they make sense for you.

Can I get a walk-in tub or roll-in shower if I rent my home?

Yes, with your landlord's written permission. Bring the landlord a written quote and product details so they know exactly what will change. Supply-only delivery can work well if the owner prefers their own contractor.

Can I use bath oils or bath salts in a jetted walk-in tub?

Check the owner's manual first. Oils and some salts can build up in jet lines and leave residue, especially in Arizona hard water. Many owners save them for tubs without jets or rinse the system with a cleaning cycle afterward.
