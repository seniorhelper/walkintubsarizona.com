Who lives here

A county where one in four people is 65 or older

In Pima County, 24.4% of residents are 65 or older, according to the Census Bureau. That is a higher share than Arizona as a whole. Many people retired here for the mountains, the dry air and the quieter pace. Others have lived in Tucson for decades and now want to stay in the home they raised a family in.

That makes bathroom safety a common topic around Tucson kitchen tables. If you are thinking about a walk-in tub or a roll-in shower, you are in good company, and there are local resources ready to help.

Tucson homes

Adobe walls, flat roofs and older plumbing

Tucson has some of the oldest neighborhoods in Arizona. Those homes have character, and they need a little extra planning.

Adobe and territorial homes

Homes in historic areas like Barrio Viejo, Armory Park and the Sam Hughes neighborhood often have thick masonry walls and small, original bathrooms. Pipes may run through the walls or the slab, so changes take care.

Mid-century block homes

Many central and east Tucson ranch homes are block construction with a standard tub space. Original steel tubs and galvanized pipes are common, and both can be replaced during the install.

Newer homes outside the city

Oro Valley, Marana, Sahuarita and SaddleBrooke have many newer single-story homes with roomy bathrooms and standard tub spaces that suit most walk-in tubs.

Measure the doorways. Older Tucson homes can have narrow doors. Many walk-in tubs are 26 to 32 inches wide, and compact models around 48 to 53 inches long exist for small bathrooms. Shower kits come in panels that pass through tight spaces.

Tucson water

Hard water here too

Like Phoenix, Tucson has hard water. Minerals collect in jet lines, heaters, drain valves and the rubber door seal. Air jets hold less water between baths than water jets, and a cleaning cycle after use helps. A water softener protects the tub and the rest of your plumbing.

Summer heat matters as well. The cold line runs warm in July, so a fast drain and an anti-scald valve make baths more comfortable.

Helping from a distance

For sons and daughters who live out of town

Many calls about Tucson and Green Valley homes come from adult children who live in another state. They see a parent struggling on a visit, or they hear about a near-fall on the phone, and they want to help without flying in for a week.

That can work well. You can handle most of the planning from wherever you live:

  • Ask a neighbor or your parent to send photos of the bathroom and doorways
  • Join the quote call by phone, together with your parent
  • Review the written quote side by side before anyone decides
  • Pick an install date when a trusted person can be at the house

Most important, let your parent lead. Ask how they bathe now, what scares them and what they would miss about their old tub. A safer bathroom works best when the person using it helped choose it.

Watch for warning signs. Bathing less often, a wet towel left on the floor to stand on, or grabbing the towel bar to get up are all signs the tub has become hard to use. They are worth a calm conversation, not an argument.

FAQ

Tucson and Green Valley questions

Can a walk-in tub go in an adobe or territorial-style home in Tucson?

Yes, in most cases. Thick masonry walls and older plumbing set in concrete can make the job slower, and some older homes have small bathrooms or narrow doors. We look at photos first and may suggest a compact tub or a panel shower kit.

How does the Southern Arizona VA help with a bathroom change?

Veterans who get care through the Southern Arizona VA Health Care System can ask their VA doctor about a HISA grant. The doctor writes the prescription, and you send VA Form 10-0103 with photos and an itemized estimate. We can provide the written estimate.

What does the Pima Council on Aging do for bathroom safety?

The Pima Council on Aging, at 520-790-7262, runs a home repair and barrier removal program for qualifying homeowners age 60 and older. Funds are limited and there is often a waitlist, so call early and ask what you need to apply.

I am a winter resident in Green Valley. Can the tub be installed while I am up north?

Yes. Many Green Valley residents arrange for a family member, neighbor or property manager to let the installers in. We confirm the date ahead of time and walk you through the tub once you are back.

Is it more expensive to get a walk-in tub delivered to SaddleBrooke or Sahuarita?

Your written quote spells out every cost, including delivery and installation, before you decide. We install anywhere in Arizona, so living outside Tucson is not a barrier. Ask us about your address and we will tell you what to expect.

Is Tucson water hard on a walk-in tub?

Tucson water is hard too, so scale can build up in jets, heaters, drain valves and door seals. Air jets, regular cleaning cycles and a water softener help keep a tub working well.
