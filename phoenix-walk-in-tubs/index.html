Know your house

Phoenix homes are not all the same, and neither are their bathrooms

The age and style of your home shape what fits, how long the job takes and what the plumber finds behind the wall.

Arcadia & Moon Valley

Roomy ranch homes

Many of these single-story homes have larger hall baths and wide halls, which makes a full-size walk-in tub easy to carry in. Some have been remodeled more than once, so we check what is under the newer tile.

Historic districts

Older plumbing, small rooms

Homes in central Phoenix historic neighborhoods can have galvanized supply pipes, cast-iron drains and narrow doors. A compact tub or a panel shower kit is often the easier fit, and old pipes may need a small repair.

Ahwatukee & North Phoenix

Newer tract homes

These homes tend to have a standard 60-inch tub space, which is the size most walk-in tubs are made for. Two-story plans raise one question: which bathroom will you use as you get older?

Phoenix summers

What a Phoenix summer means for your bath

Here is something most tub brochures never mention. In a Phoenix summer, water from the "cold" tap can come out near 100 degrees. The pipes run through hot soil, slab floors and attics, and the water heats up before it reaches you.

That matters for a walk-in tub. You sit in the tub while it fills, and you stay seated while it drains. If the water is warmer than you planned, you cannot just hop out. So in Phoenix we pay extra attention to three things:

  • A fast drain. Dual drains empty the tub quickly, so you are not waiting long once the bath is done.
  • An anti-scald valve. It holds the hot side steady. It cannot make water colder than the cold line, so we talk about timing too.
  • Your water heater setting. 120 degrees is the common safety setting. If yours is higher, lowering it is a simple fix.

A small habit helps as well. In July and August, many people bathe in the evening or early morning, when the cold line runs cooler, and keep a bath thermometer by the tub. If warm baths worry you, or if you have heart or blood pressure concerns, ask your doctor what water temperature is right for you.

Phoenix water

Hard water is the quiet enemy of jets and seals

Phoenix water is hard. City testing puts it at roughly 158 to 344 milligrams per liter, about 9 to 20 grains per gallon, with a typical level around 228. You see it as white crust on your faucets. Inside a walk-in tub, that same crust collects in places you cannot see: the jet lines, the inline heater, the drain valve and the rubber door seal.

None of this means you should skip the jets. It means choosing well and caring for the tub. Air jets push warm air rather than water through the lines, so they hold less mineral water between baths. Many tubs also run a short drying or cleaning cycle after each use.

Showers too

Roll-in showers in Phoenix: when no step is the safest step

A walk-in tub still has a low threshold to step over and a seat you lower yourself onto. For many people that is perfect. But if you use a wheelchair, a rollator or a shower chair, or if a caregiver helps you bathe, a roll-in shower is usually the better fit.

A roll-in shower replaces your tub with a low or zero-threshold base, grab bars, a fold-down seat and a handheld showerhead. Panel kits from Bestbath, made in the USA, and ANZZI come in pieces, which helps in older central Phoenix homes where doors and halls are narrow.

If you are on ALTCS, roll-in showers are the kind of bathroom change the program lists for home modifications. Ask your case manager about a needs assessment.

Install day in Phoenix

What a typical Phoenix install looks like

  1. Before the day. We confirm the tub fits through your front door and halls, and we plan around your water shut-off. In summer, we bring the tub inside quickly so it is not sitting in the sun.
  2. Out with the old. The old tub or shower comes out and the plumbing is checked. In older homes this is when we find out if a pipe needs a repair, and we tell you before doing anything extra.
  3. In with the new. The tub or shower is set, leveled, connected and sealed. Many jobs finish in as little as one day.
  4. A walk-through. We show you the door, the controls, the fast drain and the cleaning cycle, and we test it with you.

FAQ

Phoenix walk-in tub questions

My house is in a Phoenix historic district. Can you still install a walk-in tub?

Usually, yes. Historic district rules mostly cover the outside of the home, so a bathroom change is rarely an issue. Older homes can have galvanized or cast-iron pipes and small doorways, so we look at photos of the plumbing and the path to the bathroom before we quote.

Why does my bath water feel too warm in July in Phoenix?

In summer the cold line in Phoenix can run near 100 degrees, because pipes run through hot ground and attics. That means you have less cool water to mix with. A fast drain, a thermometer and a shorter fill help, and an anti-scald valve keeps the hot side from spiking.

Does the Carl T. Hayden VA help with a walk-in tub or roll-in shower?

Veterans who get care at the Carl T. Hayden VA Medical Center can ask their VA doctor about a HISA grant for bathroom changes. The doctor must prescribe it, and you send VA Form 10-0103 with photos and an itemized estimate. We can give you that written estimate.

Who can help a Phoenix homeowner who cannot afford a bathroom change?

Start with the Region One Area Agency on Aging Senior HelpLine at 602-264-4357, and ask Ability360 about its home modification program. Funds are limited and there may be a wait, so call early and ask what papers they need.

Can I get a roll-in shower in a Phoenix condo or patio home?

Often, yes. Shower kits come in panels that fit through tight halls and elevators. In a condo, check with your association about work hours and water shut-offs before the install day.

Is a water softener worth it for a walk-in tub in Phoenix?

For most Phoenix homes it helps a lot. Phoenix water is hard, and scale builds up in jets, heaters, drain valves and door seals. A softener, air jets and regular cleaning cycles keep the tub working and the seal sealing.
