Cost drivers

What moves the price up or down

Some of these are about the tub. Many are about your house.

The model

A simple soaker costs less than a tub with air and water jets, heated seats or a two-person design.

Size

Compact and standard 60-inch tubs cost less than wide bariatric models with bigger doors and higher weight ratings.

Jets

Air jets, water jets or both add pumps, blowers and controls. Choose them if you will use them often.

Door style

Outward-swing and wide transfer doors can cost more than a standard inward door.

Inline heater and fast drain

A heater keeps the bath warm. A dual or pump-assisted drain empties it sooner. Both add parts.

Electrical work

Jets and heaters usually need a dedicated GFCI-protected circuit. If your panel has no room, that adds an electrician's time.

Plumbing changes

Moving a drain, replacing an old valve or working in a concrete slab takes more labor than a straight swap.

Tile and wall repair

Removing a tub can disturb tile or drywall. Some repair is normal. Water damage behind the wall adds more.

Removal and travel

Hauling away the old tub is part of the job. Very rural or remote Arizona addresses can add travel time.

Two ways to buy

Installed price vs. supply-only price

An installed price covers the tub, delivery, removal of the old tub, setting the new one, plumbing and electrical connections, and cleanup. One company is responsible for the whole job. With us, many installs are done in as little as one day by factory-certified installers. See how an Arizona install day goes.

A supply-only price covers the tub and delivery to your home. Your own licensed contractor does the rest, and you pay them separately. This often makes sense if you already have a contractor you trust, or if you are remodeling the whole bathroom anyway. Read the details on supply-only delivery in Arizona.

To compare the two fairly, add your contractor's written labor quote to the supply-only price. In Arizona, nearly every tub job needs an ROC-licensed contractor, so make sure your installer has one. Our guide on checking an Arizona ROC license shows how in a minute.

The cheapest tub can become the most expensive one if the door, seat height or drain is wrong for you. Fit first, features second, price third.

Where markups hide

Why some in-home sales quotes run so high

Some national brands with in-home sales visits send a salesperson to sit at your kitchen table for hours. The cost of that visit, the advertising that booked it and the commission all end up in the price.

Common pressure moves include a "today only" discount, a price that drops if you sign before the salesperson leaves, or a quote that is never left in writing. None of those help you choose the right tub.

We work differently. You can get a quote by phone or from a few photos. If a measurement visit helps, it is for measuring, not selling. You get the model, the price and the timeline in writing, and you can show it to your family, your doctor or another company. Read more about how not to overpay for walk-in tubs and roll-in showers.

A fair rule of thumb: if you would not buy a car on the first visit without a written price, do not buy a bathtub that way either. A quote that will not go in writing is a quote to walk away from.

Help paying

Funding that can offset the cost

Most Arizona families pay out of pocket, but several programs are worth a phone call before you buy.

Veterans may qualify for the VA HISA grant: up to $6,800 lifetime for a service-connected condition or a 50% or higher rating, or up to $2,000 lifetime for other veterans eligible for VA care. A VA doctor must prescribe the change.

ALTCS members can ask their case manager about a roll-in shower as a home modification. Area Agencies on Aging run limited repair programs for qualifying adults 60 and older.

Medicare: Original Medicare generally does not cover walk-in tubs or shower conversions. Some Medicare Advantage plans offer limited home-safety benefits, so ask your plan.

FAQ

Walk-in tub cost, answered

Why do walk-in tub quotes vary so much between companies?

The tub itself is only part of the price. Companies that send salespeople into your home build the cost of that visit, advertising and commissions into the quote. Jets, heaters, electrical work and wall repairs also add up, so compare quotes item by item, not just the total.

Is it cheaper to buy a walk-in tub supply-only?

Supply-only costs less because you are not paying for our installation, but you still pay your own licensed contractor. Get a written labor quote from your contractor first, then add it to the supply-only price to compare fairly with an installed quote.

Does a walk-in tub add to my electric bill?

Tubs with jets or an inline heater use electricity when they run, and many need a dedicated circuit. A plain soaker uses none. The bigger cost is usually heating the extra hot water a deep tub holds.

Will a walk-in tub raise or lower my home's value?

It depends on the buyer. For many buyers it is a plus, especially in Arizona's retirement communities, while others may prefer a standard tub. If resale is on your mind, a roll-in shower or a tub that fits the original alcove keeps options open.

Can I pay for a walk-in tub over time?

Ask about payment options once you have a final written quote for the exact model and scope. Knowing the true total first gives you an honest monthly figure instead of a guess.

What should I do if a salesperson says a price is only good today?

Thank them and take your time. A fair price will still be fair next week. Ask for the quote in writing, compare it with others, and never sign the same day if you feel rushed.
