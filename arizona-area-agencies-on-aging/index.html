Arizona's Area Agencies on Aging, region by region

Eight regional agencies cover every county and tribal community in Arizona. Here is which one serves you, what they can and cannot do about a hard-to-use bathroom, and how to make the first call count.

Direct answer: Arizona has eight Area Agencies on Aging (AAAs), funded through the Department of Economic Security's Division of Aging and Adult Services. Start here

What an Area Agency on Aging actually is

Every state has Area Agencies on Aging. They exist because of the federal Older Americans Act, which sends money through each state to regional agencies that know their own communities. In Arizona, that money flows through the Department of Economic Security (DES), Division of Aging and Adult Services, which contracts with eight regional agencies. Some are nonprofits, some are part of a regional council of governments, and two serve tribal nations.

The agencies do not sell anything and they are not insurance. Think of them as the switchboard for aging services in your part of the state. A trained person listens to what is going on at home, checks what you may qualify for, and connects you with programs that are paid for, partly paid for, or simply worth knowing about.

For a family worried about bathing, that matters for three reasons. First, the agency may know of a local home repair or barrier-removal program that most people never hear about. Second, it can line up help that makes bathing safer right now, such as an in-home aide or caregiver respite, while you plan a bigger change. Third, it can tell you whether you should be applying for ALTCS, Arizona's Medicaid long-term care program, which can pay for some home modifications when a case manager puts them in the service plan.

Plain truth first: an Area Agency on Aging rarely pays for a walk-in tub. Where money for home changes exists, it is usually small, limited to people with low incomes, and aimed at basics like grab bars, ramps or a safer shower. It is still worth the call, because the agency can open doors you would not find on your own.

Check the county on your property tax bill if you are unsure.

Services

What they can help with, as it relates to bathing

Each agency builds its own menu, so the exact programs differ from Mohave County to Santa Cruz County. These are the services that come up most when a bathroom has become hard to use.

Information and referral

A specialist matches your situation to local programs, including ones run by cities, churches and charities, not just the agency's own.

Case management

For people with more complex needs, a case manager assesses daily living tasks such as bathing and builds a plan of services.

In-home help

Personal care or housekeeping aides, where funded, can assist with bathing while a long-term fix is planned.

Caregiver support

The Family Caregiver Support Program offers training, counseling and respite for family members who do the caregiving.

Home repair or adaptation

Some regions fund small repairs or barrier removal for qualifying homeowners. Funds are limited and often waitlisted.

Medicare counseling

Free, unbiased help comparing Medicare plans, including whether a Medicare Advantage plan offers any home-safety benefit.

The DES describes these as non-medical home and community based services delivered through the local agencies. Respite for family caregivers, for example, is generally limited to caregivers of someone who needs substantial help with at least two activities of daily living, such as bathing and dressing, and the caregiver's own risk is assessed as well.

Before you dial

How to make the first call count

Agency phone lines are busy, and the first conversation shapes what happens next. A few minutes of preparation help the specialist understand the situation quickly.

  1. Write down the bathing problem in one sentence. For example: "My mother can no longer step over her tub wall and has slipped twice since spring."
  2. List the daily tasks that need help. Bathing, dressing, toileting, getting in and out of bed or a chair, walking. Be honest; this is how eligibility is judged.
  3. Have basic facts ready. Age, county, whether the person owns or rents, rough monthly income, and any coverage such as AHCCCS, ALTCS, Medicare Advantage or VA care.
  4. Ask three direct questions. Is there a home repair or adaptation program in this region? Is there a waitlist, and how long is it? Should we also be applying for ALTCS?
  5. Write down names and dates. Note who you spoke with and what they said they would do. If you are a long-distance caregiver, share the notes with siblings.

If the agency points you toward a home repair program, ask what paperwork it needs. Programs that fund bathroom changes usually want a written, itemized estimate. We provide that for free, along with product specifications and photos, so the request is not held up by a vague quote.

Other routes

When the agency is not the right door

An Area Agency on Aging is one of several places to look, and for some families it is not the most direct. Here is how the main options line up in Arizona.

  • Veterans: start with your VA primary care team. The VA's Home Improvements and Structural Alterations (HISA) grant can help with medically needed bathroom changes when a VA physician prescribes them. Our VA and ALTCS funding guide covers the amounts and steps.
  • People with low income who need nursing-home level care: ask AHCCCS about ALTCS. Home modifications can be covered when a case manager writes them into the service plan, and an accessible shower usually fits that policy better than a tub. Read how to ask ALTCS for a roll-in shower.
  • People with Medicare: Original Medicare generally does not pay for bathroom remodels, walk-in tubs or grab bars. Check the Medicare equipment coverage page, and call your plan if you have Medicare Advantage.
  • Family caregivers: the Arizona Caregiver Coalition runs a resource line at 888-737-7494 and has offered respite reimbursement in recent years. Ask what is available now.
  • Taxes: some medically needed home changes can count as medical expenses. IRS Publication 502 explains the rules; talk with a tax professional before you count on it.

Snowbirds

Arizona agencies and seasonal residents

Thousands of Arizonans live here only part of the year. Agency services are generally for residents of the region, so a winter visitor whose legal home is in another state may be told to start with the agency back home. If you split your year and plan to age in place in Arizona, say so on the call and ask how residency is decided. For timing a bathroom project around a seasonal schedule, see our snowbird timing guide.

The best time to call an aging agency is before a fall, not after one. Waitlists are shorter for the people who are already on them.

FAQ

Area Agency on Aging questions

Does an Arizona Area Agency on Aging pay for walk-in tubs?

Rarely. Where an agency or a partner runs a home repair or barrier-removal program, the money is usually limited, aimed at people with low incomes, and spent on basics such as grab bars, ramps or a safer shower. Ask your regional agency what exists now and whether there is a waitlist.

How do I find the Area Agency on Aging for my county?

Arizona has six county-based regions plus two agencies serving tribal communities. Maricopa County is Region One, Pima County is the Pima Council on Aging, NACOG covers Apache, Coconino, Navajo and Yavapai, WACOG covers La Paz, Mohave and Yuma, Central Arizona Aging covers Pinal and Gila, and SEAGO covers Cochise, Graham, Greenlee and Santa Cruz. The DES website keeps the current list.

Who can use Area Agency on Aging services?

Most services are for adults 60 and older, and caregiver programs serve the family members who help them. Some programs also serve younger adults with disabilities. Each program has its own rules, so the agency checks eligibility on the call.

Should I call the agency where I live or where my parent lives?

Call the agency for the county where your parent lives. Services are delivered locally, so the agency near the home is the one that can help.

Can the agency help while we wait for a bathroom change?

Often, yes. Depending on funding, an agency may arrange in-home personal care, caregiver respite or training while a longer-term fix is planned. Ask what short-term help is available for bathing specifically.

Will you provide paperwork for an agency or grant program?

Yes. We provide a free, itemized written quote, product specifications and photos, which is what most programs ask for.
