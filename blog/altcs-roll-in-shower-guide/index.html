What ALTCS is, in plain words

ALTCS stands for the Arizona Long Term Care System. It is part of AHCCCS, Arizona's Medicaid program, and it helps people who need ongoing long-term care. Many ALTCS members live at home rather than in a nursing facility, with services like in-home help, adult day programs and, in some cases, changes to the home itself.

Those home changes are called home modifications. AHCCCS describes them in its medical policy manual, in a section called AMPM Policy 1240-I. The policy explains that a home modification is a physical change to the home that has a specific adaptive purpose. In plain words, the change has to help the member live more safely or independently because of their needs, not just make the home nicer.

Source: AHCCCS Medical Policy Manual, AMPM 1240-I Home Modifications.

The bathroom is one of the most common places where a change helps. Getting in and out of a tub can become unsafe long before someone is ready to leave their home. For ALTCS members, a roll-in shower is often the path that fits the program best.

Showers vs tubs

Why showers usually fit ALTCS better than tubs

The AHCCCS policy lists roll-in showers and walk-in showers with handheld showerheads as the kind of bathroom change it covers. Walk-in tubs are not typically listed. That does not mean every shower request is approved or every tub request is denied, but it does tell you where the program is pointed.

There are practical reasons. A roll-in shower has no step at all, so it works for someone who uses a wheelchair, a shower chair or a walker, and for a caregiver who helps with bathing. A handheld showerhead lets the member or caregiver wash while seated. As needs change, a barrier-free shower usually keeps working.

A walk-in tub still has a low step at the door, and the bather has to sit while the tub fills and drains. For many people that is fine and even a joy, but it is a harder fit with a policy built around adaptive purpose.

The process

How an ALTCS home modification request works

Each health plan handles details its own way, but the main steps look like this.

  1. Talk with your case manager. Every ALTCS member has a case manager. Tell them bathing has become unsafe and you would like to ask about a home modification. Be specific: "I cannot step over the tub wall" is clearer than "the bathroom is hard."
  2. Needs assessment. The plan looks at your needs, often with input from a doctor, nurse or occupational therapist. The assessment connects the change to your health and daily living.
  3. Service plan. If the modification is approved, it is written into your service plan, the document that lists your ALTCS services. Ask for a copy so you know what was approved.
  4. Quotes and contractor. The plan explains how contractors are chosen and how many quotes are needed. Do not sign a contract or pay a deposit until your case manager says it is okay.
  5. Installation and sign-off. After the work, the plan may check that it was done as approved. Keep all paperwork and photos.

Important: starting work before approval can mean the plan will not pay. Always wait for written approval from your health plan or case manager.

Good questions

What to ask your case manager

About the process

What are the steps with our health plan? Who does the assessment? How long does each step usually take? Will I get the decision in writing?

About contractors

Does the plan have contractors it works with? Can I suggest one? How many quotes do you need? Does the contractor need an Arizona ROC license number on the quote?

About the shower

Does the plan want a roll-in or a walk-in shower? Should the quote include grab bars, a seat and a handheld showerhead? What about the bathroom door?

If the answer is no

What was the reason? Can I add more information? How do I ask for a review or appeal, and what is the deadline?

Avoid delays

Why some requests stall, and how to keep yours moving

Most delays come from missing pieces. You can head off many of them before you start.

The need is not clear

"The bathroom is old" does not explain an adaptive purpose. Describe what you cannot do safely, like stepping over the tub wall or bathing from a wheelchair.

The quote is too vague

A one-line price makes reviewers ask for more. An itemized quote that lists each part of the work answers their questions up front.

Missing permission

Renters need written landlord approval, and some HOAs want notice. Get those early so they do not hold up the final step.

Lost follow-up

Case managers handle many members. A friendly call every couple of weeks, with your notes in hand, keeps your request from slipping.

Other help

If ALTCS is not the right fit

Not everyone qualifies for ALTCS, and not every request is approved. Other programs may help:

  • Veterans may qualify for VA home improvement grants for a bathroom change. A VA physician must prescribe the change.
  • Area Agencies on Aging can point you to local help. In Maricopa County, the Region One Senior HelpLine is 602-264-4357. In Pima County, the Pima Council on Aging is 520-790-7262.
  • Medicare: Original Medicare generally does not cover walk-in tubs or shower conversions. Some Medicare Advantage plans offer limited home-safety benefits, so ask your plan.

For families

A note for caregivers

If you are helping a parent or spouse through ALTCS, you already know the paperwork can feel endless. Keep one folder, paper or digital, for the bathroom request: notes from each call with the date and the name of the person you spoke with, copies of the assessment and service plan, the quote and the photos. When a question comes up months later, that folder is gold.

Ask for every decision in writing, and keep a copy of every document you hand over.

FAQ

Questions Arizona families ask

Who is eligible for ALTCS?

ALTCS is Arizona's Medicaid long-term care program for people who meet both medical and financial requirements, such as needing a nursing-home level of care. AHCCCS decides eligibility, not a contractor. If you are not yet enrolled, AHCCCS or your local Area Agency on Aging can explain how to apply.

Can ALTCS pay for a walk-in tub instead of a shower?

Walk-in tubs are not typically listed as a covered home modification in the AHCCCS policy, while roll-in and walk-in showers with handheld showerheads are. Your case manager and health plan make the final decision, so ask them directly. If a tub is what you want, ask whether other funding such as a VA grant could help.

How long does an ALTCS home modification take?

It varies by health plan and by how complete the request is. The assessment, approvals, quotes and scheduling each take time, so it is wise to start early. Ask your case manager for the expected steps and timeline in your plan.

Does my contractor need to be approved by the health plan?

Health plans often work with specific contractors or have rules about who can do covered work. Ask your case manager before you sign anything or pay a deposit. Starting work before approval can mean the plan will not pay for it.
