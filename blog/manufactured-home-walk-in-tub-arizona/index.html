Walk-in tubs in Arizona manufactured homes and park models

By Aging Safely Baths · Updated October 10, 2026 · 8 min read

Manufactured homes, mobile homes and park models are a big part of Arizona's retirement housing, from Mesa and Apache Junction to Yuma, Lake Havasu City and Camp Verde. They can take a safer tub or shower, with a few extra checks.

Context

A common Arizona home, with its own rules

Arizona has a large share of manufactured housing, especially in retirement areas. Mesa and Apache Junction have long rows of manufactured-home communities and RV resorts with park models. Yuma, Lake Havasu City, Bullhead City and Quartzsite fill with winter residents who live in them for half the year. Rural towns from Camp Verde to Benson have many manufactured homes on private lots.

These homes can be very comfortable places to age. Many are single-story with no steps inside, which is a real advantage. But they are built differently from site-built houses, and a bathroom change needs a slightly different checklist. The questions below are the ones we work through before recommending a walk-in tub or a shower.

Home type

Know which kind of home you have

The terms get used loosely, but the differences matter for a bathroom project.

Your data plate, usually inside a kitchen cabinet or the primary bedroom closet, and the HUD label on the exterior tell you the manufacturer and the year. Photos of both help us check what we are working with.

The big question

Floor support and water weight

Water is heavy: about 8.3 pounds per gallon. A walk-in tub that holds 50 gallons carries more than 400 pounds of water, plus the tub itself and the person bathing. Deeper or larger tubs hold more. In a site-built home on a slab, that is rarely a concern. In a manufactured home, the bathroom floor sits on joists over a crawlspace, and the weight lands on a fairly small area.

That does not mean a walk-in tub is impossible. It means someone needs to look at the floor and what supports it before you order. Depending on what we find, the options include:

  • Adding support under the tub, such as extra blocking or piers, installed by a licensed contractor.
  • Choosing a compact or lower-capacity tub that spreads less weight over the floor.
  • Choosing a low-threshold shower instead, which weighs far less when in use and often fits the existing space more easily.

Soft spots are a warning. If the bathroom floor already feels soft or bouncy near the tub, there may be water damage underneath. That needs to be repaired before any new fixture goes in, whatever you choose.

Fit

Doors, halls and small bathrooms

Doorways and hallways in manufactured homes and park models are often narrower than in site-built houses, and bathrooms are often smaller. Many walk-in tubs are 26 to 32 inches wide, and they arrive in one piece, so the path from the front door to the bathroom has to be wide enough with every turn.

  1. Measure the exterior door and every interior doorway on the way, with the door fully open.
  2. Measure the hallway width and note any tight turns.
  3. Measure the existing tub or shower space: length, width and height to any window.
  4. Take a photo of the bathroom from the doorway, showing the tub, toilet and sink.

If the path is tight, a shower kit that arrives in panels, or a compact tub, often solves it. Our Fit Check turns these numbers into a starting recommendation.

Utilities

Water heaters and electrical service

Manufactured homes often have smaller water heaters than site-built homes, and some are tucked into an exterior closet. A deep tub can use most of a small tank, leaving a lukewarm bath. Before choosing a large tub, check the tank size on the water heater label. A smaller tub, an inline heater to hold temperature, or a shower can all help.

Walk-in tubs with air jets, whirlpool jets or heaters usually need one or two dedicated GFCI-protected circuits. Older electrical panels in manufactured homes may not have room. A licensed electrician should confirm the panel can handle the new circuits. A soaking-only tub or a shower needs no new circuits.

State rules

Arizona rules for altering a manufactured home

In Arizona, the Department of Housing's Office of Manufactured Housing oversees the installation of manufactured homes and certain changes to them. State law makes it unlawful to alter a manufactured home unless you hold the right license or certification, and the state's administrative rules call for plan approval before an alteration. Whether a particular bathroom project counts as an alteration depends on what changes, such as structural framing, plumbing or electrical, so check with the Office of Manufactured Housing or your installer before work starts.

Sources: Arizona Department of Housing, Office of Manufactured Housing; Arizona Administrative Code R4-34-701.

Whoever does the work should hold an Arizona Registrar of Contractors license in the right classification. You can check any ROC license in about a minute.

Communities

Park rules and rented lots

If your home sits on a rented lot in a manufactured-home community or an RV resort, the park has its own rules. In Arizona, many of these relationships are governed by the Arizona Mobile Home Parks Residential Landlord and Tenant Act, along with the park's written rules. Ask the park office about contractor access, work hours, parking for a delivery truck and debris removal before install day. Decision

Tub or shower in a manufactured home?

Here is the honest summary. A walk-in tub can work well in many manufactured homes when the floor is sound or reinforced, the path is wide enough and the water heater keeps up. In a park model or an older mobile home, a low-threshold shower with a seat, grab bars and a handheld sprayer is usually the better fit: lighter, easier to deliver, faster to install and simpler to maintain in hard water. FAQ

Manufactured home questions

Can a walk-in tub go in a manufactured home in Arizona?

Often, yes, but check the floor support, the doorways and the water heater first. A tub that holds 50 gallons carries more than 400 pounds of water before anyone sits in it, so the floor under the bathroom matters.

Is a shower better than a walk-in tub in a park model?

Usually. Park model bathrooms are compact, and a low-threshold shower is lighter, easier to deliver and faster to install. A walk-in tub rarely fits a park model without major changes.

Do I need a permit to change the tub in my manufactured home?

It depends on what changes. Arizona's Department of Housing regulates alterations to manufactured homes, and its rules call for plan approval before an alteration. Ask the Office of Manufactured Housing or your installer whether your project counts.

My water heater is small. Will a walk-in tub still work?

A deep tub can use most of a small tank. Check the tank size, and consider a smaller tub, an inline heater to keep the water warm, or a shower instead.

Does my park have to approve the work?

Most parks and RV resorts have rules about contractor access, hours, parking and debris. Check with the park office before install day.

Do you remove the old tub?

Yes. The crew removes and hauls away the old tub or shower, so nothing is left at your home or in the park.
