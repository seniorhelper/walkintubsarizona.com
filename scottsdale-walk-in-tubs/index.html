Aging in place, beautifully

You chose this home for a reason

Maybe it is the view of Camelback Mountain or the McDowells. Maybe it is the patio where the grandkids swim, or the bathroom you remodeled just the way you wanted. Whatever the reason, most people want to stay right where they are. An AARP survey found that 76% of adults 50 and older want to stay in their current home.

The worry we hear most in Scottsdale is not cost. It is looks. People fear that a safer bathroom will feel clinical, with chrome rails and white plastic where marble used to be. It does not have to.

A walk-in tub or curbless shower can be planned like any other part of a fine bathroom: chosen for its lines, its color and its finish, and placed where it makes sense.

Design choices

Four ways to make safety look intentional

Small decisions make a big difference in how a walk-in tub or shower looks in a finished bathroom.

Match your metal finishes

Faucets, handheld showerheads and grab bars come in several finishes. Matching them to your cabinet pulls and light fixtures makes the whole room feel planned.

Choose grab bars that look like towel bars

Many grab bars are made to look like decor. Placed well, they do double duty, and guests will not know they are there for safety.

Pick a tub color that fits

Walk-in tubs come in more than one color. A warm white or biscuit shade can blend with desert stone and cream tile better than bright white.

Finish the surround

Tile, stone or solid panels around the tub make it look built in. Your designer can match what you already have.

The soak

A walk-in tub can be a true soaking tub

A walk-in tub is deeper than most standard tubs, and you sit upright on a built-in seat, so the water reaches your shoulders. For many people that deep, warm soak is the best part of the day.

Hydrotherapy models add air jets, water jets or both. Many people find warm water and jets soothing for sore joints and tired muscles. Some models add a heated seat or backrest, so you stay warm while the tub fills and drains. Ask your doctor first about heat, blood pressure and circulation.

The modern option

Curbless showers suit the Scottsdale look

Large-format tile, frameless glass and no curb to step over: that is a modern bathroom, and it is also a safe one. A roll-in or curbless shower lets you walk in, or roll in, with nothing to trip on. Add a fold-down teak-style seat, a handheld showerhead on a slide bar and a grab bar that matches your faucets.

Prefabricated shower bases from Bestbath, made in the USA, and ANZZI give you a sloped, watertight floor. Your tile setter can then finish the walls to match the rest of the room.

Before you buy

Questions worth asking any walk-in tub company

A beautiful brochure tells you little about how a tub will feel in your bathroom years from now. Whoever you talk to, including us, these questions help you compare honestly:

  • Does the door open inward or outward, and which works better in my room?
  • How high is the seat, and can I stand up from it without pushing hard?
  • How long does the tub take to fill and to drain?
  • Are the jets air, water or both, and how are they cleaned in hard water?
  • What finishes and colors are available for the tub and the trim?
  • Who installs it, and what is their Arizona ROC license number?
  • Is the quote in writing, with every item listed?

Hard water, even here

Fine finishes need care

Scottsdale water is hard, like most of the Valley. White mineral spots show up fast on dark tile, glass and matte black fixtures. A water softener, a squeegee for the glass and a soft cloth for the tub door seal keep a design-forward bathroom looking new. Air jets and a cleaning cycle protect the tub's inner parts. FAQ

Scottsdale walk-in tub questions

Can a walk-in tub match the finishes in my Scottsdale bathroom?

Often, yes. Many walk-in tubs come in more than one color, and faucets and grab bars come in finishes like brushed nickel, chrome and oil-rubbed bronze. Send us photos of your bathroom and we will suggest models and trims that blend in.

Can my own interior designer or remodeler install the tub?

Yes. We can ship the walk-in tub or shower supply-only to your home and share specs with your designer and your ROC-licensed contractor. That works well when the tub is part of a larger bathroom remodel.

Is a walk-in tub a good fit for a large primary suite in Paradise Valley?

It can be. In a big bathroom, many people keep the walk-in tub as the soaking spot and add a curbless shower nearby. The key is to plan the path between them, with grab bars and a non-slip floor.

What should I ask my doctor before using a hydrotherapy walk-in tub?

Ask whether warm baths are safe for you, how warm the water should be and how long you should soak. Heat can affect blood pressure and circulation. Many people find warm water and jets soothing, but your doctor knows your health best.

Do you serve Cave Creek and Fountain Hills, or only Scottsdale proper?

We serve all of them, and every other part of Arizona. Homes farther out, like in Cave Creek or Rio Verde, just take a little more planning for delivery day, and we confirm the timing with you in advance.

Will a curbless shower look out of place in a Scottsdale home?

Usually the opposite. Curbless showers with large tiles and glass are a popular modern design. Built with a slight slope, a linear drain and a fold-down seat, they look sleek and are easy to roll or walk into.
