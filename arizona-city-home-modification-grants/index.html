City bathroom grants in Arizona, checked one by one

Which city programs may help pay for a safer shower, and who qualifies.

In short: a few Arizona cities run their own home modification money, separate from VA and ALTCS. Chandler offers a grant of up to $10,000 for people with disabilities, including shower changes. Tucson's Home Access Program targets about $15,000 per home (capped at $20,000) for owners 62 or older or disabled. Most programs are income-limited, owner-only and first come, first served.

These programs rarely show up when families search for "walk-in tub grants," because the cities file them under housing rehabilitation, not bathrooms. We read each city page on November 17, 2026 and wrote down what it actually says, including the parts that rule people out. How far does a city grant go on a roll-in shower?

A city grant can cover a real share of a bathroom, which is more than most families expect. Read the chart with two cautions. First, these are ceilings. A program pays what its inspector writes up for your house, and the Tucson figure is a target, not a promise.     What rules knock people out of city programs?

    Most families who are turned down are not turned down for income. They hit one of the smaller rules that sit lower on the city page. These are the ones we saw again and again:

    • You have to own the home and live in it. Tucson, Chandler, Mesa and Gilbert all say so. A parent living in an adult child's house usually does not qualify in the parent's name.
    • Taxes and mortgage must be current. Tucson and Chandler both require it, and Mesa adds utilities.
    • Reverse mortgages. Chandler's program excludes homes with a second or reverse mortgage, which matters in a city with many retirees.
    • Mobile homes. Chandler excludes them. Tucson's Housing Repair Program accepts them, and Tucson runs a separate PRICE program for mobile homes in certain neighborhoods, though its list of repairs does not include accessibility.
    • ALTCS. Tucson's Home Access and Housing Repair programs are not available to ALTCS recipients.
    • Timing. Tucson ranks its repair waitlist by severity and warns that it is not for emergencies. Mesa's program is paused. Apply before the bathroom becomes urgent, not after a fall.

    What if your city is not on the list?

    Plenty of Arizona cities and towns have no program of their own, and unincorporated areas such as Green Valley fall under county programs instead. Three routes work almost anywhere in the state:

    • The Arizona Family Caregiver Reimbursement Program. According to AARP Arizona, it reimburses family caregivers 50% of qualifying home modification and assistive technology costs, up to $1,000, for a relative 18 or older who needs help with daily activities such as bathing. Funding is limited and first come, first served; call the Caregiver Resource Line at 888-737-7494 to check current status.
    • USDA Section 504 in rural areas. Owners 62 and older in eligible rural areas may get a grant of up to $10,000 (lifetime) to remove health and safety hazards, and loans of up to $40,000 at a fixed 1% rate. Grants must be repaid if the home is sold within three years.
    • Your Area Agency on Aging. Regional agencies often know about nonprofit repair programs that never appear in a web search. Our Arizona Area Agencies on Aging guide lists the agency for every county.

    Veterans have their own lane on top of all of this. The VA's HISA benefit tops out at $6,800 per lifetime when the need is service-connected (or the rating is 50% or more) and at $2,000 for other enrolled veterans; spa and jet features are excluded. See our VA bathroom contractor page for how that paperwork runs. For a national view of every program that pays toward a tub or shower, Aging Safely Baths keeps a running list of grants and programs that really pay for a walk-in tub.

    How do you apply without losing months?

    1. Call the city line first and ask three things: is the program open, is there a waitlist, and does it cover a shower or tub change for accessibility in your case.
    2. Get a doctor's or therapist's note that names the need, such as "unable to step over tub wall safely." Programs that want proof of disability ask for something like it.
    3. Gather the paperwork most programs list: deed, ID, income for everyone in the home, a recent utility bill, and proof that taxes are current.
    4. Ask whether you may use your own contractor. If yes, check the installer's Arizona ROC license (our ROC license guide takes two minutes) and attach an itemized quote.
    5. Do not start work before approval. Grant programs generally do not pay for work already done.

    FAQ

    Arizona city grant questions

    Does any Arizona city pay for a walk-in tub or roll-in shower?

    Some city programs may help. Chandler offers a grant of up to $10,000 that names shower changes, Glendale lists shower modifications through Habitat for Humanity Central Arizona, and Tucson's Home Access Program funds barrier removal with a target of $15,000 per home. Each has income, ownership and residency rules.

    Who qualifies for the Tucson Home Access Program?

    Owner-occupied homes inside Tucson city limits, with household income at or below 80% of area median income and at least one member 62 or older or disabled who needs a modification for safe access. Taxes and mortgage must be current, and ALTCS recipients are not eligible.

    Is the Mesa home repair program open?

    Not as of November 17, 2026. The City of Mesa says its Emergency Rehabilitation Program is on hold and not accepting new applications, but residents can fill out an interest form to be contacted when it resumes. Its grant goes up to $30,000 per household per year.

    Can I rent and still get a city home modification grant?

    Usually not. The Tucson, Chandler, Mesa and Gilbert programs require that you own the home and live in it. Renters with a disability can ask the landlord for a reasonable modification, and family caregivers may qualify for Arizona's caregiver reimbursement of up to $1,000.

    Can I use a city grant and VA HISA together?

    Sometimes, but each program sets its own rules about other funding and about who chooses the contractor. Ask both before work starts. HISA covers up to $6,800 per lifetime for a service-connected need or $2,000 for other enrolled veterans, never spa or jet features.
