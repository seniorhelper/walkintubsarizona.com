Building a casita for a parent? Start with the bathroom.

Arizona's 2024 ADU law opened the door. Here is how to get the shower right.

In short: since Arizona's HB 2720 was signed on May 21, 2024, cities with more than 75,000 residents must allow accessory dwelling units on single-family lots, up to 75% of the main house or 1,000 square feet, whichever is less. For a parent moving in, the bathroom decides whether the casita works for years or only until the next fall.

We get this call more each winter, often after a holiday visit when a family sees how hard the stairs or the old tub have become. The good news is that a new casita is the easiest place in Arizona to build a truly step-free bathroom, because the floor has not been poured yet. The bad news is that most casita plans are drawn as guest rooms, with a standard tub and a 24-inch door, and fixing that after the slab is poured costs far more than drawing it right.

What does Arizona's casita law allow?

HB 2720 applies to cities with more than 75,000 people, which takes in Phoenix, Tucson, Mesa, Chandler, Gilbert, Glendale, Scottsdale, Peoria, Tempe, Surprise and other large cities. Smaller towns and unincorporated county land set their own rules, so check with your building department either way.

The bathroom

What should a casita bathroom for an aging parent include?

Private homes are not required to meet the ADA, but the 2010 ADA Standards are the best-tested set of numbers for a bathroom that works with a walker, a wheelchair or a helper. We use them as a planning checklist, not a legal requirement. These are the dimensions worth putting on the casita plans before anyone prices the job:

A few choices matter more in an Arizona casita than in the main house:

  • Recess the shower floor before the pour. Most Arizona casitas sit on a concrete slab. A curbless shower needs the pan area dropped so water runs to the drain without a lip. Asking for that on the foundation plan costs little; cutting a finished slab later is a different job.
  • Block the walls for grab bars now. Wood blocking behind the drywall at 33 to 36 inches lets you add bars anywhere later, even if the parent does not need them yet.
  • Think about hot water distance. A detached casita at the back of the lot can mean a long wait for hot water, and a parent who stands in a cold shower waiting is more likely to rush. Plan a dedicated water heater or a recirculating line with your plumber.
  • Set the water heater to 120°F. The CPSC recommends it to reduce scald risk: most adults suffer third-degree burns from 140°F water in about 6 seconds, but at 120°F it takes about 5 minutes. The CPSC notes that most scald victims are older adults and young children.
  • Keep the path level. The walk from the main house to the casita door matters as much as the shower. A flush threshold at the casita entry beats a ramp you have to add later.

Shower or tub

Roll-in shower, transfer shower or walk-in tub?

In a new casita we usually point families toward a shower, because it can grow with the parent's needs. A roll-in shower takes a shower wheelchair or a helper. A transfer shower suits someone who sits to bathe and slides across from a wheelchair. A walk-in tub fits a parent who still walks and wants a warm soak for sore joints, and it drops into a standard 60-inch alcove.

For an existing bathroom, installed pricing depends on the room and the model, and we quote it in writing. In new construction your builder handles framing and plumbing rough-in, so the shower or tub may be bought supply-only and set by the builder's crew. Our supply-only delivery page explains how that works, and builders who want a one-piece pan sized to the slab recess can browse barrier-free shower bases at Showers4Less.

Is there help paying for a casita bathroom?

Help for new construction is thinner than help for remodeling an existing bathroom, so set expectations early. A few routes are worth checking:

  • Tucson's ADU Improvement Program. In May 2023 the City of Tucson announced a pilot that helps low-income owners improve an existing, permitted ADU, including upgrading bathrooms. It does not fund new construction, and the city did not list amounts, so call Housing and Community Development to ask whether it is open.
  • City home modification grants. Programs such as Chandler's grant of up to $10,000 apply to the home where the person with a disability lives. Our guide to Arizona city home modification grants lists what each city offers and who qualifies.
  • VA housing grants. Some veterans with serious service-connected disabilities can use SAH or SHA grants, which can pay for adapting a home. See our VA bathroom contractor page for limits and how builders work with VA.

FAQ

Casita bathroom questions

Can I build a casita for my parent in Arizona?

In cities with more than 75,000 residents, HB 2720 requires that single-family lots allow at least one attached and one detached accessory dwelling unit. Smaller towns, county land and HOA covenants can set other rules, so check with your building department and your association first.

How big can an Arizona casita be?

Cities covered by HB 2720 must allow an ADU of at least 75% of the main home's floor area or 1,000 square feet, whichever is less. They may allow more. Lot coverage, height and private HOA covenants still apply.

What size roll-in shower should a casita have?

The 2010 ADA Standards list 30 by 60 inches as the minimum standard roll-in shower and 36 by 60 inches as the alternate, with a threshold no higher than 1/2 inch. For a parent who may need a helper or a shower wheelchair, planning 36 by 60 inches or larger gives more room.

Do private casitas have to meet the ADA?

No. The ADA Standards apply to public accommodations and certain other buildings, not to a private home or casita. Many families still use the ADA figures as a planning checklist because they are well tested for walkers, wheelchairs and caregivers.

Should a casita get a walk-in tub or a roll-in shower?

For new construction we usually suggest a roll-in or transfer shower, because it adapts as mobility changes and a slab can be recessed for a curbless entry. A walk-in tub suits a parent who still walks and wants to soak. Installed pricing depends on the room and the model, so ask for a free written quote.
