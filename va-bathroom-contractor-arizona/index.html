In short: a VA HISA grant pays up to $6,800 over a veteran's lifetime for a service-connected condition (or a 50% or higher rating) and $2,000 otherwise, and VA pays the veteran, not the contractor. Your contractor's job is a written, itemized estimate that lists labor, materials, permits and inspections, because federal rules require exactly that before VA will approve the work.

That last point surprises a lot of Arizona veterans. Most people expect the VA to hire the contractor, or at least to send the check to one. Under the HISA rules in 38 CFR 17.3100 to 17.3130, the veteran (the "beneficiary") applies, VA approves, and VA pays the beneficiary, either as a 50% advance or after the work is finished. Picking a contractor who already knows how that paperwork runs saves weeks.

What does the VA need from your contractor for a HISA grant?

A complete HISA application has five parts. Two of them come from your contractor, and one of the others depends on them:

  • A prescription written or approved by a VA physician, naming the improvement and the medical reason for it.
  • VA Form 10-0103, signed, with an optional request for an advance payment.
  • The property owner's approval, signed (and notarized if the veteran is not the owner).
  • A written, itemized cost estimate covering labor, materials, permits and inspections. This is the contractor's piece.
  • A color photo of the area before the work. We take these during the quote and send them with the estimate.

If anything is missing, VA tells the applicant and closes the application if the documents do not arrive within 30 days. VA will not approve a HISA application until it has done a pre-award inspection of the home or decided one is not needed. A vague estimate ("bathroom remodel, $9,000") is the most common reason a packet stalls, so ours lists each item on its own line.

Source: 38 CFR 17.3120, HISA application; VA Prosthetic and Sensory Aids Service, HISA.

The money path

How does HISA money actually get paid?

VA can send a 50% advance to the veteran within 30 days of approval, if the veteran asks for it on Form 10-0103. Only one advance is allowed per approved application, and it can be spent only on the work described.

When the job is done, the veteran sends a final payment request within 60 days of approval (or of the advance). It needs a statement that the work is complete, a color photo of the finished work, and itemized actual costs for materials, labor, permits and inspections. VA may inspect within 30 days and decides within 30 days. It then pays the lesser of the approved amount or the actual cost, minus any advance. Unused money goes back to the veteran's lifetime HISA balance.

One protection is worth knowing: if an inspection finds the work unfinished, VA will not try to collect the advance when the veteran documents that the contractor was at fault, for example through bankruptcy or misconduct.

Source: 38 CFR 17.3130, HISA benefit payments; 38 CFR 17.3125, approval.

Honestly, it usually covers part of the job, not all of it. Aging Safely Baths publishes installed prices of $13,500 to $17,000 for roll-in showers and $15,250 to $20,000 for walk-in tubs after current promotions. Here is how those ranges compare with the two HISA limits:

Two more rules shape the plan. HISA does not pay for spa, hot tub or jacuzzi features, so jets on a walk-in tub are not a HISA item. And because the limit is lifetime, a veteran who used part of it on an earlier ramp or grab bars has less left. The difference can come from savings, financing, an ALTCS or Area Agency on Aging program, or a larger VA housing grant if the veteran qualifies for one.

Bigger projects

What about SAH and SHA contractors in Arizona?

Specially Adapted Housing (SAH) and Special Housing Adaptation (SHA) grants are run by VA's home loan side, not the medical center, and they are much larger. VA.gov lists these maximums as of November 17, 2026:

SAH (FY2027)Up to $133,042Certain severe service-connected disabilities, such as ALS, loss of use of more than one limb, or blindness in both eyesBuilder registers with VA for a Builder ID and works through the veteran's SAH agent: bids, plans, cost breakdown, compliance inspections
SHA (FY2027)Up to $26,654Certain service-connected disabilities, such as loss of use of both hands or certain severe burns or respiratory injuriesSame SAH agent process, on a smaller project

SAH and SHA grants can be used up to six separate times over a lifetime, and the application is VA Form 26-4555. VA's builder training lists the steps a builder goes through: bid submission, VA contract requirements, plans and specifications, a cost breakdown and disbursement schedule, compliance inspections, a final field review, escrow and lien releases, and change orders. The veteran's SAH agent is the point of contact for all of it.

Where we fit: on an SAH or SHA renovation, the bathroom is often one part of a larger job led by a VA-registered builder. We can supply the roll-in shower or walk-in tub with full specifications and an itemized price for the builder's bid, or install the bathroom portion where the SAH agent and builder agree. Ask your SAH agent how your project is structured before you request bids.

Where do Arizona veterans start?

For HISA, start with your VA primary care team, because the prescription has to come from a VA physician. In the Valley that is usually the Phoenix VA (Carl T. Hayden VA Medical Center), in southern Arizona the Southern Arizona VA Health Care System in Tucson, and in the north the Bob Stump VA Medical Center in Prescott. The prosthetics office at each one handles HISA paperwork. Our Arizona funding guide lists all three and the programs that can fill the gap.

Before anyone starts work, check that the contractor holds an active Arizona Registrar of Contractors license. Our guide to checking an ROC license shows how in two minutes.

FAQ

VA bathroom contractor questions

Does the VA pay the contractor directly for a HISA bathroom project?

No. Under 38 CFR 17.3130, VA pays the veteran. The veteran can ask for a 50% advance after approval and receives the rest after sending a final payment request with a photo and itemized actual costs. The veteran then pays the contractor.

What estimate does the VA need for HISA?

A written, itemized estimate of costs for labor, materials, permits and inspections, along with a color photo of the area before the work. The full application also needs a VA physician's prescription, VA Form 10-0103 and the property owner's signed approval.

Can I start the bathroom work before HISA is approved?

Wait for approval. VA approves a HISA application only after a pre-award inspection or a decision that none is needed, and the approved work is what the payment covers. Starting early risks paying for work the grant will not reimburse.

Will HISA pay for a walk-in tub with jets?

HISA does not pay for spa, hot tub or jacuzzi features. A walk-in tub or roll-in shower may qualify when a VA physician prescribes it, but jets and similar features have to be paid another way, so we list them separately on the quote.

How much are SAH and SHA grants now?

VA.gov lists fiscal year 2027 maximums of up to $133,042 for SAH and up to $26,654 for SHA, as of November 17, 2026. VA can adjust these each year based on construction costs, and the grants can be used up to six times over a lifetime.
