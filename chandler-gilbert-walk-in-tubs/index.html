Start with the big question

Which bathroom should become the safe one?

In a single-story home the answer is easy. In the two-story tract homes common across Chandler, Gilbert and Queen Creek, it takes a little thought.

Option 1

The upstairs primary bath

It is the bathroom you use now, and it often has the most room. But a walk-in tub upstairs only helps while you can manage the stairs. Choose this if stairs are easy for you today and likely to stay that way.

Option 2

A downstairs full bath

Many East Valley floor plans have a downstairs guest room with a full bath, often with a tub and shower combo. This is usually the smartest spot. Pair it with the guest room and you can live on one floor if you need to.

Option 3

A downstairs half bath

If there is only a powder room downstairs, a small addition or a closet borrowed from a nearby room can sometimes make space for a roll-in shower. It is a bigger job, so get a written plan first.

Think a few years ahead

Plan for the body you will have, not just the one you have today

People often buy for how they feel this year. That makes sense, but a bathroom change lasts a long time. Ask yourself three questions:

  • If I had knee or hip surgery next year, which bathroom could I reach?
  • Is there a bedroom, or a room that could become one, on the same floor?
  • Would I rather soak in a tub or sit and shower?

If you are not sure how you would answer, the two-minute Fit Check walks you through it and points you toward a walk-in tub or a roll-in shower.

About garden tubs. Many homes built in the 1990s and 2000s have a wide garden tub in the primary bath. Those tubs sit in a tiled deck and are a different shape from a walk-in tub, so swapping one takes more work. Often the separate shower next to it is the easier place to make safer.

Many generations, one house

A bathroom that works for grandparents and grandkids

Many families in Chandler and Gilbert share a home across three generations. A parent moves into the downstairs guest room. Grandkids come and go. One bathroom may need to serve someone who uses a walker and someone who still needs help washing their hair.

That is good news for a safe bathroom, because the features that help an older adult help everyone. A handheld showerhead is easier for bathing a toddler. A seat makes shaving legs simpler. Grab bars steady a teenager on a wet floor as much as a grandparent.

A walk-in tub works well when the person who needs it loves to soak and can sit and stand from a chair. A roll-in shower works better when a caregiver helps, or when a wheelchair or shower chair is part of the routine.

HOAs

Your HOA and install day

Most of Chandler, Gilbert and Queen Creek is HOA country. The good news: most HOAs do not review work inside your home. The things they do care about tend to show up on install day:

  1. Trucks and parking. Some HOAs limit work trucks on the street or in shared lots.
  2. Work hours. Many set quiet hours, especially on weekends.
  3. Old fixtures. Rules may cover dumpsters or leaving debris at the curb. Our installers haul away the old tub.
  4. Proof of license. Some ask for the contractor's Arizona ROC license and insurance. We provide both.

FAQ

Chandler and Gilbert questions

Should I put the walk-in tub upstairs or downstairs in my two-story home?

If stairs are already hard, or may be in a few years, choose a downstairs bathroom near a room you can sleep in. A tub upstairs only helps while you can still climb the stairs safely. If there is no full bath downstairs, a roll-in shower in a converted downstairs bath is often the answer.

Do I need HOA approval for a walk-in tub in Gilbert or Chandler?

Most HOAs do not review work inside your home, but many have rules about dumpsters, work trucks, parking and work hours. Check your HOA rules or call the management company before install day so nothing gets flagged.

Can I replace the garden tub in my primary bathroom with a walk-in tub?

Often, yes, though it is more work than a standard tub swap. Garden tubs sit in a deck, usually in a different size than a walk-in tub, so the deck, tile and plumbing may need changes. Many people find the separate shower in that same bathroom is the easier spot for a low-threshold shower instead.

Will a walk-in tub work for my parent and my kids at the same time?

Yes. Many families use the walk-in tub for a grandparent and the handheld showerhead to bathe small children while seated. A roll-in shower also works well for everyone, from toddlers to a parent who uses a walker.

What is it like to get a walk-in tub in Sun Lakes?

Sun Lakes is a 55+ community with its own rules, so check with your homeowners association about contractor access and work hours. Most homes are single-story, which makes choosing the bathroom easier.

How do I know if my Tempe home's plumbing is ready for a walk-in tub?

Older Tempe homes may have aging pipes and drains, which can slow a walk-in tub's drain or need a repair. Send us photos of the tub and the access panel or plumbing wall, and we will tell you what to expect before we quote.
