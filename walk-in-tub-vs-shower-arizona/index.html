Walk-in tub or shower: which fits your Arizona home?

By Aging Safely Baths · Updated October 10, 2026 · 8 min read

The right answer depends on how you move, how you like to bathe and how your household lives here. This guide compares the three main options with Arizona conditions in mind.

Direct answer: For Arizona homeowners, a walk-in tub is usually best if you can sit and stand from a chair-height seat and want to soak. A low-threshold shower suits people who can manage a small step but want the simplest routine, and a roll-in shower suits walkers, wheelchairs and caregivers who need room. Hard water, summer tap temperatures and part-year living can tip the choice, so weigh them too.

Step one

Start with the body, not the bathroom

Brochures tend to start with features. A better place to start is with three honest questions about the person who will bathe there, today and a few years from now.

  1. Can you sit down on a chair-height seat and stand back up without help? A walk-in tub has a seat around chair height, but you still have to stand up from it after the bath.
  2. Can you step over a few inches safely? Every walk-in tub door has a low step-in threshold. Low-threshold showers have a small lip. Roll-in showers have essentially none.
  3. Do you want to soak, or do you mainly want to get clean safely? If warm water for sore joints matters to you, a tub earns its place. If not, a shower is usually simpler.

If the answers are yes, yes and soak, a walk-in tub is a strong candidate. If stepping is the problem, or a walker or wheelchair is part of daily life, a shower is usually the safer path. Our two-minute Fit Check asks the same questions and adds your measurements.

Local

Arizona factors that change the answer

Most comparison guides stop at the table above. In Arizona, four local conditions often decide close calls.

Hard water

Water in much of Arizona is hard, and mineral scale builds up in anything that holds or moves water. A walk-in tub with water jets, an inline heater and a fast-drain valve has more parts that can scale than a simple shower. That does not rule out a tub. It means choosing air jets over water jets, running cleaning cycles and possibly adding a softener. If you want the lowest-maintenance option, a shower wins. Our hard water guide explains the care routine.

Summer tap temperatures

In July, the cold line in many Arizona homes runs warm, so a bath you mix the same way all year can turn out hotter in summer. Sitting in a deep tub of warm water in a warm house can leave some people lightheaded when they stand. A shower lets you adjust and step away more easily. If you choose a tub, an anti-scald valve, a thermometer and a slow stand-up help. See Arizona heat and bathroom safety.

Part-year living

If you spend summers elsewhere, a walk-in tub sits unused for months. That is fine, but it is worth draining it fully, running a cleaning cycle before you leave and again when you return. Showers simply need the drain traps topped up. Our snowbird timing guide covers when to order and install around a seasonal schedule.

Single-story homes and 55+ communities

Many Arizona retirement homes are single-story with two bathrooms. That opens a third option: do both. Couples often turn one bathroom into a walk-in tub for the person who loves to soak and the other into a low-threshold or roll-in shower. If your community has an association, interior work rarely needs design approval, but check contractor rules first. Our HOA and 55+ guide has a checklist.

Price

What drives the cost of each

We do not publish prices, because they vary too much from one home to the next to be useful as a single number. These are the factors that move the price for each option, so you can ask good questions when you compare written quotes.

  • Walk-in tub: the model and size, the door style, jets and heaters, the electrical work for those features, removal of the old tub, and any wall, tile or plumbing repair.
  • Low-threshold shower: the base and wall system, the valve, any seat and grab bars, and how much of the old tub surround must come out.
  • Roll-in shower: everything above, plus how the floor is made level or recessed for a curbless entry, the drain location, and water control at the bathroom floor.

Our Arizona cost guide goes deeper, and the funding guide explains how VA grants and ALTCS can help, along with what Original Medicare generally does not cover.

Small steps first

When the answer is "not yet"

Sometimes the right first step is not a remodel. If bathing has just started to feel risky, a few changes can make a real difference while you decide: a sturdy grab bar anchored into the wall, a handheld shower on a slide bar, a non-slip mat, a shower chair or a tub transfer bench, and better lighting. An occupational therapist can recommend exactly what helps. Our bathroom fall prevention guide lists what to check, room by room.

The safest bathroom is the one that matches how you move on your worst day, not your best one.

FAQ

Tub or shower questions

Is a walk-in tub safer than a walk-in shower?

It depends on the person. A walk-in tub lets you bathe seated, which helps people who tire or feel unsteady standing, but it still has a step and you must stand up from the seat afterward. A low-threshold or roll-in shower avoids most or all of the step, which is usually safer for walkers, wheelchairs and anyone who struggles to lift a foot.

Which option is easier to maintain with Arizona hard water?

A shower has fewer parts that hold water, so it usually needs less upkeep. A walk-in tub with jets, a heater and a fast-drain valve benefits from air jets, regular cleaning cycles and possibly a water softener.

Can I have both a walk-in tub and a shower?

Yes, if you have two bathrooms or a large one. Many Arizona couples convert one bathroom to a walk-in tub and the other to a low-threshold or roll-in shower.

Is a roll-in shower only for wheelchair users?

No. A roll-in shower has no step, so it also suits people who use a walker, shuffle when tired or bathe with help from a caregiver. Many people choose one to plan ahead.
